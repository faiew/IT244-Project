The following assumptions were made during the design of the Smart Clinic Database System
where the project requirements did not specify detailed business rules.
1. Each appointment is linked to exactly one patient and one staff member. Group appointments
and appointments handled by multiple staff members are outside the scope of this project.
2. One appointment may result in more than one treatment record if multiple procedures are
performed during the same visit.
3. Payments are linked to appointments rather than treatments, and an appointment may have
more than one payment to support partial or installment payments.
4. The PRESCRIPTION entity is used to resolve the many-to-many relationship between
TREATMENT and MEDICINE by introducing an associative entity.
5. Every doctor, nurse, and receptionist must first exist in the STAFF table before being assigned
to their corresponding subtype.
6. Each staff member belongs to one department, while a department can have multiple staff
members.
7. System-generated IDs are used as primary keys instead of attributes such as phone numbers or
national IDs because these values may change over time.
8. Medicine stock is assumed to be updated through SQL operations or database triggers during
the implementation stage.
9. Appointment scheduling conflicts, such as double-booking a staff member, are assumed to be
handled by the application and are not enforced in the database design.
